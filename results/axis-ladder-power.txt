p is against a permutation null -- the length a slope of this grid reaches over
these diffs with the offsets shuffled, so with no value in them. |axis| on its own
says nothing: least squares returns a slope through any cloud. rel is split-half
reliability, how much of the fitted direction is signal. A rung needs both, on both
objectives: a large axis at low reliability is a long vector pointing nowhere in
particular, and reliability without length is a direction with nothing along it.


=== one axis or two? ===

    rung  cos(a0,a1)       p  disattenuated    dim2   reading
   50.1M      -0.025   0.244              —   0.487   no axis to ask of
   60.1M      -0.277   0.000         -0.613   0.361   mostly one knob
   70.1M      -0.560   0.000         -0.809   0.220   mostly one knob
  140.2M      -0.760   0.000         -0.885   0.120   one knob

The raw cosine is attenuated toward zero by noise in both axes, so it understates
collinearity wherever reliability is poor -- which is every early rung. Disattenuating
divides that out, and is refused below a reliability of 0.2 rather than printed as a
number the data cannot support.

The distinction that matters: dim2 near 0.5 with a cosine near zero is what TWO value
registers look like AND what two noise vectors look like. The table above is what
separates them. Only a rung with an axis can be said to have one axis or two.

'writes' means the 95% intervals at the two extreme writes are disjoint and the
agent still finishes its episodes -- the edit moved the trade-off further than the
measurement's own uncertainty. The random column is a norm-matched direction of the
same length and should not move: if it does, the rung is measuring perturbation size
rather than the axis. 'base cannot do the task' is not a verdict about the axis --
an agent that does not reach objectives has no trade-off to write to.

Earliest rung whose axis writes: 140.2M (novalue11.s1234)

Read the tables in order. The first says which rungs have an axis at all, and no 
later table means anything on a rung it did not clear. The second says how collinear 
the two objectives' axes are once that is established. The third says whether the 
axis yet points where it ends up. The write table is the only one that leaves the 
weights, and is what an axis has to survive to be called one.
