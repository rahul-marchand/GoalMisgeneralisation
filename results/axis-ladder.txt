p is against a permutation null -- the length a slope of this grid reaches over
these diffs with the offsets shuffled, so with no value in them. |axis| on its own
says nothing: least squares returns a slope through any cloud. rel is split-half
reliability, how much of the fitted direction is signal. A rung needs both, on both
objectives: a large axis at low reliability is a long vector pointing nowhere in
particular, and reliability without length is a direction with nothing along it.

The raw cosine is attenuated toward zero by noise in both axes, so it understates
collinearity wherever reliability is poor -- which is every early rung. Disattenuating
divides that out, and is refused below a reliability of 0.2 rather than printed as a
number the data cannot support.

The distinction that matters: dim2 near 0.5 with a cosine near zero is what TWO value
registers look like AND what two noise vectors look like. The table above is what
separates them. Only a rung with an axis can be said to have one axis or two.
