1. THE RELIABILITY COLUMN IS NOT A SHAPE EFFECT. IT IS THE LEARNING RATE.

  grouped by the arm learning rate the calibration chose:
    3e-5  n=4   reliability 0.891  [0.873, 0.910]
    1e-4  n=2   reliability 0.830  [0.784, 0.876]
    3e-4  n=3   reliability 0.479  [0.368, 0.676]
  grouped by depth, which is what the campaign is about:
    L=4   n=3   reliability 0.896  [0.876, 0.910]
    L=8   n=3   reliability 0.647  [0.394, 0.873]
    L=16  n=3   reliability 0.677  [0.368, 0.879]

  correlation(log lr, reliability) = -0.856. The learning-rate groups separate
  with non-overlapping ranges; the depth groups overlap almost completely.

  Worse, SEVEN OF NINE CELLS CHOSE AN ENDPOINT of the 3e-5/1e-4/3e-4 ladder, so
  for most of the grid the best rate was outside the range searched. The warning
  built into pick_arm_lr.py for exactly this fired almost everywhere and was, in
  hindsight, the result.

  The calibration was introduced (Preregistration-scaling.md, amendment of
  2026-08-22) to remove the confound that one fixed rate is a different-sized
  step at different widths. It removed that one and introduced a larger one.
  A ladder spanning 10x in three steps cannot resolve a quantity this sensitive.

2. WHAT SURVIVES: THE WRITE, AND IT GOES THE OTHER WAY

  Write error does not track the learning rate -- 1.43, 1.90, 1.28 across the
  three rate groups, no order -- and falls monotonically with depth:
    L=4  2.10   L=8  1.29   L=16  1.05     (1.70 at L=4 excluding the one
                                            cell below 0.99 competence)
  Both parameter-matched pairs agree, deep member better:
    3.17M   (256,4) 1.85  vs  (128,16) 0.89
    12.6M   (512,4) 1.55  vs  (256,16) 1.03

  P2 and P5 predicted depth would break the write before the read. FALSIFIED,
  and in the opposite direction: depth improved the write.

3. P4: DELOCALISATION IS REAL, AND ONE BLOCK WAS NEVER ENOUGH

  modules that must be written to land within 2 steps of the full axis:
    L=4   3-4 of 4 blocks      best single block off by 4.1-4.7 steps
    L=8   5-7 of 8 blocks      best single block off by 5.3-5.8 steps
    L=16  7-14 of 16 blocks    best single block off by 5.6-5.8 steps

  The count grows with depth, about 0.65L, which is the registered prediction.
  But the first half of P4 is wrong: a single block never sufficed, at any
  depth, including the shallowest. The axis was never localised to one block.

  As a FRACTION of blocks the requirement falls (88%, 75%, 66%), so "more
  layers must be written at greater depth" is true in absolute terms and false
  in relative ones. The practically relevant claim -- one layer is never enough
  -- holds everywhere.

  Enrichment (share of ||axis||^2 against share of parameters) RISES with depth,
  1.22 at L=4 to about 1.8 at L=8 and L=16, so the axis is more concentrated
  relative to chance in deeper models even as its largest single share falls
  from 0.30 to 0.11. Raw share and enrichment point opposite ways; enrichment is
  the one that is comparable across shapes.

4. P3 CANNOT BE ANSWERED, AND THE REASON IS AN INSTRUMENT FAILURE

  Participation ratio against its permutation null, every cell:
    observed 3.03 vs null 3.02, 3.16 vs 3.14, 10.03 vs 9.19, ...
  No cell shows excess rank. On this evidence the family is rank one, which is
  the registered prediction and the task's true rank.

  The residual reliability statistic says nothing. Its correlation with the axis
  reliability is +0.989: it measures whether ANY direction replicated, not
  whether a SECOND one did. A cell whose fine-tune was noisy has low residual
  reliability for the same reason it has a low axis reliability.

  029's own docstring has this exactly backwards. It says to read the
  replication figure rather than the participation ratio, because noise inflates
  the ratio. The data says the opposite: the ratio WITH ITS NULL is informative
  and the replication figure WITHOUT a null is not. A fixed threshold (P3's
  0.30) was the mistake; the permutation null was the thing that worked.

5. NOT TESTED

  The train/test gap by capacity -- the memorisation prediction registered in
  the 2026-08-22 amendment, and the reason the layout split was built at all --
  was never instrumented. Every --eval set passed to training is held out; no
  training-set evaluation was ever run, so the gap cannot be computed from what
  is on the volume. It needs one extra evaluation set per cell and a re-run of
  the evaluation pass, not of the training.

  Competence saturates: eight of nine cells sit at or above 0.99 optimal, so
  most of the grid is above the regime where the task discriminates at all.
