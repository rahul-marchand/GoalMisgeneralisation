Three things this settles.

1. REMAT IS REQUIRED, AND CHEAPER THAN BUDGETED. With it off, (512,16) at batch
   1024 does not fit in 80 GB at all -- it OOMs, which is the memory estimate
   confirmed from the other side. Where it does fit, remat costs 22% at (128,4)
   (41.5 -> 50.7 ms) and 6.8% at (512,4) (208.4 -> 222.5 ms), against the 33%
   the plan assumed. Peak memory falls by about 3.3x at both.

2. BATCH SIZE DOES NOT CHANGE EFFICIENCY. 20.9, 22.4 and 23.1 TFLOP/s at batch
   256, 1024 and 4096 for (128,4); 66.0 and 69.4 at 256 and 1024 for (512,16).
   The workload was never launch-bound, so the argument that a larger batch
   would buy MFU was wrong. Batch 1024 is kept because fewer, larger steps cost
   nothing, not because it is faster.

3. THROUGHPUT IS SET BY WIDTH ALONE. 22.5 TFLOP/s at every depth for d=128,
   40.5 for d=256, 69 for d=512. Depth buys proportional time and no efficiency.
   Even the widest cell reaches 7% of the card's ~990 TFLOP/s peak: with 186
   tokens and an unfused attention the GEMMs are too narrow to fill an H100, and
   no scheduling change fixes that.

The consequence for the campaign is that the nine bases plus arms are about 30
GPU-hours, not the 5 the plan assumed -- roughly $100 at $3.29/hr rather than
$30. Cost is what it is whatever the card count, since it buys FLOPs; extra
cards buy wall-clock down to the (512,16) base's own 7.3 h, which is the floor.
